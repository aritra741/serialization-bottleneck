P4  centroid by offset band  (this dataset's shortcut control)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Band = the quartile of the centroid's distance from the bbox centre,
  in bbox-diagonal units. In band 0 the bbox centre IS the centroid to
  within 2%, so answering the bbox centre scores. In band 3 it is more
  than 7% away and the centroid has to be computed. Band 3 is primary.
  Bands are held at 26/26/25/23 per tier, so a tier difference in
  centroid accuracy cannot come from the shortcut getting easier.

      band       offset     n      acc             95% CI
         0    0.00-0.02    66     37.9       [25.8, 50.0]
         1    0.02-0.04   114     36.0       [27.2, 44.7]
         2    0.04-0.07    96     35.4       [26.0, 44.8]
         3       > 0.07    24     37.5       [20.8, 58.3]
  free ride band 0 - band 3 = +0.4 pp   p = 0.9780
  A large positive gap means most of the centroid score was the
  bbox-centre shortcut rather than a computed centroid.

  band 3 only, by tier (the honest centroid number):
        tier     n      acc             95% CI
      simple     8     87.5      [62.5, 100.0]
      medium     8     25.0        [0.0, 62.5]
        hard     8      0.0         [0.0, 0.0]
  n is ~8 per tier in band 3, so these three points carry very wide
  intervals and a swing between them is not by itself an effect.

  the two centroid shortcuts, as published by the dataset:
        tier  bbox centre  coord mean    model
      simple         75.0        62.0     53.0
      medium         73.0        84.0     35.0
        hard         78.0        88.0     21.0
  new_data_v7 publishes both; a model has only to find whichever works
  better, so the bar it must clear is the larger of the two.

  READ THIS. In new_data_v7 the COORD-MEAN shortcut is the larger one,
  and unlike every other balanced quantity in this dataset it RISES
  with tier (0.62 / 0.84 / 0.88 at 5%, against 0.35 / 0.29 / 0.37 in
  new_data_v6). v7's samplers space vertices evenly round the outline,
  and the mean of an evenly spaced ring converges on the true centroid
  as the count grows - so this shortcut gets BETTER exactly where the
  task is meant to get harder. offset_band does not catch it: that
  balances the OTHER shortcut, distance from the bbox centre.
  Any hard-tier centroid accuracy below the coord-mean rate is a
  model underperforming an answer that never read the polygon.
      simple: model 53.0% vs coord-mean 62.0%  -> BELOW
      medium: model 35.0% vs coord-mean 84.0%  -> BELOW
        hard: model 21.0% vs coord-mean 88.0%  -> BELOW

P4b  area and perimeter by fill band  (the v6-run artefact this fixes)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  fill_band is the quartile of how much of its bounding box the polygon
  fills (edges 0.52 / 0.62 / 0.70). It is held at 14/37/36/13 in every
  tier. In the previous run one model answered roughly the bbox area on
  simple polygons and roughly the inscribed-ellipse area on hard ones,
  and round many-vertex polygons made the ellipse guess work - which
  showed up as a tier effect it was not. Reading area across fill bands
  separates the two.

  doing the work on the polygons that happen to fill their box.
